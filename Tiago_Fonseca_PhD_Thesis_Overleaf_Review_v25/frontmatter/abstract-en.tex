\frontmatterchapter{Abstract}

Optimising the operation of Renewable Energy Communities (RECs) requires the coordinated management of distributed generation, storage and flexible demand, including electric-vehicle charging. The objective is to reduce energy costs and increase the use of local renewable generation while satisfying participant requirements and electrical constraints. Variability in production and consumption, temporal dependencies and changes in asset availability shape this optimisation problem. Community-wide decisions introduce further complexity by coupling the operation of individual buildings through shared resources and collective objectives.

Despite advances in optimisation and learning-based energy management, the application of Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) to real-world systems, such as renewable energy communities, raises unresolved challenges of scalability, deployability, safety and robustness. This thesis investigates how these requirements can be addressed jointly when system configurations change, measurements are delayed or incomplete, and policies execute on heterogeneous computing resources.

The proposed approach adopts decentralised control, with a local agent assigned to each participating building. An integrated research platform (EnergAIze) provides the experimental and operational foundation for the investigation, connecting data, simulation, controller development and deployment. A simulation environment (CityLearn v3), together with annual datasets at 15-minute resolution, supports reproducible evaluation of heterogeneous RECs under changing configurations and operating conditions. A common evaluation protocol compares rule-based control, optimisation, single-agent Reinforcement Learning (RL) and MARL across these settings.

The principal algorithmic contribution is a learning method that accommodates changes in the control interface, termed Typed-Interface Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (TI-MARL). A versioned typed contract specifies the entities observed and controlled; a deterministic compiler translates this description into the current observations and action groups, including their dependencies and bounds. This representation enables a policy shared across agents to operate locally as assets are reconfigured within known semantic types. An analytic projector enforces local action feasibility, while a variant with centralised training, Typed-Interface Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimisation (TI-MAPPO), uses a critic over the active agent population to support coordination during training.

The evaluation combines energy performance with assessments of reconfiguration, fault response, computational scalability and deployment compatibility, using two energy demonstrators (OPEVA and DemFlex) as application contexts. These contributions integrate REC energy optimisation with the preservation of control semantics from simulation to policy execution, supporting the application of MARL to distributed, heterogeneous and reconfigurable energy systems.

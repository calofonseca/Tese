\chapter{General Discussion and Answers to the Research Questions}
\label{chap:discussion}
\markboth{Discussion and Answers to the RQs}{Discussion and Answers to the RQs}

This thesis started from a practical difficulty. A controller that performs well in a simulator is still several steps away from controlling a distributed physical system. Its observations must retain the same meaning outside the experiment, its actions must remain valid for the assets that are currently available, physical and service constraints must be enforced, and the complete decision path must survive software, hardware, and organisational change. These conditions become harder when participants, chargers, vehicles, sessions, measurements, and computing targets do not remain fixed.

The work addressed that difficulty through one cumulative argument. EnergAIze established the system boundaries and common contracts. The simulator made heterogeneous assets, reconfiguration, failures, and physical limits reproducible. The controller progression identified what rule-based, optimisation-based, single-agent, and multi-agent methods solve under increasingly demanding conditions. TI-MARL then changed the policy-facing object from a fixed vector into a compiled, entity-bound interface. The lifecycle infrastructure connected experiments to heterogeneous compute, policy export, target-specific inference, monitoring, and recovery. OPEVA and DemFlex provide the real-world contexts in which the assumptions and limits of those contributions are examined.

The discussion does not select one algorithm as a universal winner. It asks what the combined evidence establishes, which mechanism produced each result, and how far that result can be carried beyond the evaluated setting. This distinction matters in a thesis that contains software verification, controlled simulation, real-data simulation, target-runtime evidence, integration with external platforms, and bounded physical operation. These evidence levels answer different questions.

\section{What the Combined Evidence Establishes}
\label{sec:discussion-argument}
\label{sec:discussion-synthesis}

Five bodies of evidence support the thesis argument.

The simulator establishes that the conditions motivating the research can be represented together and audited. The annual dataset suite contains heterogeneous residential and service members, several asset combinations, multiple chargers per member, EV sessions, deferrable loads, local settlement, total and per-phase limits, health events, and changes in membership and asset availability. The simulator work is important because a policy cannot be credited for robustness or reconfiguration when those conditions are absent from the environment. Its tests and audits support claims about representation, execution, and reproducibility; they do not by themselves support a claim about controller quality.

The controller progression establishes that complexity must earn its place. Rule-based controllers provide transparent feasibility and strong references. Optimisation exposes the objective, constraints, and value of information. Single-agent RL tests adaptation without the coordination burden. MADDPG and MATD3 introduce decentralised continuous multi-agent control and address part of the instability observed during learning. Behaviour cloning, guided exploration, replay strategies, offline learning, distillation, and hierarchy tackle data use, training effort, reuse, and the separation of local and community decisions. Their contribution is both methodological and diagnostic: each stage identifies a problem that can be solved without assuming that every later mechanism is already necessary.

TI-MARL establishes a different form of controller contract. The audited implementation compiles a versioned member schema and current runtime evidence into typed observations, dynamic action-group instances, health-dependent validity, bounds, dependencies, and local constraints. One shared actor processes these compiled local interfaces and produces a hybrid action bundle for the groups that exist at that step. TI-PPO uses a local typed critic; TI-MAPPO retains the same actor and runtime path while estimating value from the active member set. The analytic projector applies local service and electrical constraints before command encoding. The focused regression suite provides implementation evidence for these semantics, dynamic known-type composition, topology-aware trajectories, checkpoint restoration, health closure, and requested--projected--applied traces. It does not replace the multi-seed control comparison.

The lifecycle work establishes that experiment execution and policy serving can share a governed hand-off without forcing every computing target to adopt the same infrastructure. The Job Orchestrator retains persistent job identity and state. Worker adapters execute the same scientific payload through local Docker, the personal workstation, Deucalion, Union, and Jetson-compatible paths. Algorithms exports validated ONNX or rule-based bundles for the general inference path. The deployment backend catalogues bundles and activates a selected version on a target-specific inference service, while the UI exposes state, logs, health, and operator-controlled reversal. The evidence supports heterogeneous execution and per-target bundle management. It does not yet support atomic TI-MARL policy-set rollout, automatic KPI-triggered rollback, or production certification of the typed actor bundle.

The demonstrators establish why the separation between these layers is necessary. OPEVA combines residential and corporate charging, photovoltaic generation, storage, site limits, external platforms, local controllers, vehicle information, prediction, simulation, and learned control. The reported four-building study found approximately 9\% lower daily peak and 5\% lower energy cost in its evaluated setting \parencite{fonseca2025realworldrec}. The later 17-installation real-data simulation reported reductions of 10.2\% in grid energy demand, 13.6\% in energy cost, 20.7\% in peak demand, and 30.1\% in ramping, together with a 14.2\% improvement in renewable self-consumption. Those results retain the baseline, data-processing, action-authority, and evidence-level boundaries stated in Chapter~\ref{chap:real-world}. They are not evidence of sustained autonomous control across every OPEVA site. DemFlex is analysed as a separate body of evidence and is not populated with OPEVA assumptions.

\section{Answer to RQ1: Dynamic and Heterogeneous Multi-Agent Control}
\label{sec:discussion-rq1}

RQ1 asks how MARL control can accommodate heterogeneous and changing populations, capabilities, and availability while balancing local and collective objectives. The answer developed in this thesis is to make the current control interface part of the decision process instead of hiding it in a permanent vector layout.

The formulation separates the member, the physical asset, the EV, and the charging session. These identities have different lifetimes and cannot safely be collapsed into one index. A member can remain active while a charger becomes unavailable. One charger can serve several EV sessions over time. A service building can expose several independently controlled chargers. The typed interface records these relations and associates every observation, action group, bound, health rule, and constraint with the entity to which it applies.

At runtime, the Typed Interface Compiler constructs one deterministic local snapshot from the versioned schema and current system evidence. The shared actor encodes the observations that exist and decodes a mode and bounded fraction for each active action group. A newly active instance of a known charger type reuses the same semantic encoder and group head; the network does not gain a new output coordinate. Stable member identities preserve survivor trajectories when members join or leave, while EV-session rebinding changes the relevant entity relation without forcing an unnecessary structural rebuild.

The multi-agent component is retained through the joint physical transition, shared resources, local and community reward terms, authorised aggregate observations, and, in TI-MAPPO, a central set critic used only during training. Execution remains decentralised. A member does not need the raw private state of every other participant. This boundary makes the information assumption visible and allows TI-PPO and TI-MAPPO to be compared without changing the deployed actor.

Local and collective objectives cannot be reduced to aggregate cost. The evaluation therefore includes EV service, deferrable completion, individual cost outcomes, the number of members that improve, peak, ramping, renewable self-consumption, import, export, and residual constraint pressure. A community improvement that depends on a small group receiving worse service fails the intended interpretation even when total cost decreases.

The answer is bounded to known semantic and action-group families. TI-MARL can admit new instances and tested combinations of those families; it does not infer the meaning or safety requirements of an arbitrary unknown device. An unsupported type is rejected or routed to an explicit fallback. This is a controlled form of plug-and-control, not unrestricted zero-shot control.

\section{Answer to RQ2: Multi-Dimensional Scalability of Learning and Coordination}
\label{sec:discussion-rq2}

RQ2 asks under which conditions learning and coordination can maintain useful control as population, heterogeneity, reconfiguration, and environment complexity increase under constrained resources. The results support a conditional answer. Scalability is an operating envelope with several axes, not a property that can be assigned from agent count alone.

The simulator contributes data formats, annual scenarios, memory corrections, structured interfaces, and repeatable event injection. The experiment infrastructure separates scientific scale from campaign throughput: several workers can reduce wall-clock time without changing the complexity of the controller. The deployment targets add another dimension because a policy that trains successfully on a remote GPU may still fail the memory, latency, image, or architecture requirements of an edge device.

The controller mechanisms address complementary bottlenecks. Decentralised execution limits runtime dependence on a central joint action. Parameter sharing prevents the number of actor parameters from growing with every known asset instance. The hierarchical typed encoder aggregates observations by channel and sensor before constructing group context. Dynamic group heads avoid a preallocated universal output vector. TI-MAPPO's set critic accepts a variable active population without rebuilding a fixed concatenated input. Packed evaluation and cached structural preparation reduce repeated runtime work.

None of these mechanisms makes cost constant. More observations and action groups still require more encoding and decoding work. A larger active population increases critic and simulator work. Topology events add compilation and trace cost. Training sample requirements can grow even when parameter count does not. The appropriate evidence reports control quality together with compiler, projector, actor, and critic latency; training throughput; memory; trace volume; communication; and checkpoint size.

The answer to RQ2 is therefore expressed as the largest tested combination that satisfies the declared control and resource gates, together with the bottleneck encountered next. Core-15 supports rapid development, Core-30 isolates nominal, safety, health, and dynamic conditions, and Premium-100 combines scale, heterogeneous members, several chargers, reconfiguration, faults, and physical pressure. Results obtained in one profile do not imply the same envelope under another hardware target, communication pattern, or control interval.

\section{Answer to RQ3: Safe and Robust Operation}
\label{sec:discussion-rq3}

RQ3 asks how physical and service constraints, uncertainty, faults, reconfiguration, and the difference between training and use can be incorporated and assessed. The evidence supports a layered answer in which policy learning, deterministic feasibility, physical protection, and monitoring retain separate responsibilities.

The simulator represents total and per-phase limits, asset bounds, charging and departure requirements, deferrable deadlines, outages, data problems, communication failures, device unavailability, and changes in topology. This establishes the conditions under which safety and robustness can be evaluated. It also makes the disturbance type, duration, severity, and affected scope part of the experiment record.

TI-MARL uses the same typed semantics in observation construction, action validity, dynamic bounds, health closure, and local feasibility. The actor proposes a raw hybrid bundle. The analytic local projector then enforces validity, EV service floors, V2G reserve, mandatory deferrable starts, local total headroom, and local per-phase headroom. The command and trace retain the raw, projected, and applied values. Projector intervention can therefore be measured instead of being hidden inside the environment.

Health handling is scoped through declared dependencies. Stale vehicle information can contract the affected EV actions without disabling an unrelated stationary battery. A failed charger can remove its group while leaving the member active. Loss of a critical main meter can force a wider degraded mode. Recovery uses explicit hysteresis so that a fluctuating signal does not repeatedly enable and disable control. These rules convert several robustness cases into observable interface changes.

The guarantee remains local and conditional on the correctness of the encoded model. The current TI-MARL implementation contains no cross-member hard corrector for a shared feeder or community limit. Shared constraints are exposed, learned, and evaluated from the applied trajectory; a residual violation is a failed outcome. Device protection and the external Fail-Safe Supervisor retain authority in physical operation. A low violation count after frequent external correction is evidence that the assurance layer worked, not that the policy learned safe behaviour.

Robustness is reported through degradation and recovery, not a single nominal score. Relevant outcomes include service loss, violation energy, invalid requests, fallback duration, unnecessary shutdown, recovery time, and the difference between requested and executed actions. A method is robust within the tested disturbance envelope when it maintains the declared service and safety gates or degrades in the controlled manner specified by the experiment.

\section{Answer to RQ4: Governed and Deployable MARL Lifecycle}
\label{sec:discussion-rq4}

RQ4 asks which abstractions and mechanisms allow coordinated learned control to be reproduced, governed, and deployed across heterogeneous and changing edge systems. The implemented answer begins with lineage and ends with an observed target state.

An experiment is identified by its resolved configuration, source and image revisions, simulator and dataset, scenario, interface, reward, seed, target, logs, checkpoints, results, and KPI analysis. The Job Orchestrator maintains the persistent job state. Workers translate a common payload into the execution contract of local Docker, the personal workstation, Deucalion, Union, or Jetson-class infrastructure. Target-specific authentication, scheduling, staging, images, and result recovery do not alter the scientific identity of the job.

The general inference path packages the policy with the information needed to reconstruct its input and output semantics. The manifest records topology, ordered observations, encoders, actions, bounds, algorithm format, and artefact paths. The inference service validates the bundle, rebuilds preprocessing, selects the declared agent, performs ONNX or rule-based execution, maps outputs to named bounded actions, and exposes health and active-bundle identity. The backend catalogues bundles and loads a selected bundle into a configured target. Previous versions remain available for operator-controlled reversal.

TI-MARL contributes an algorithm-side deployment contract through resolved interfaces, compiler state and rules, compatibility signatures, stable identities, actor-only bundles, and execution traces. The current generic inference service does not yet certify that typed actor bundle as a production serving path. The distinction prevents a research checkpoint from being described as deployed merely because its weights can be serialised.

The demonstrated lifecycle is strongest for reproducible experiment execution, heterogeneous worker adaptation, explicit remote recovery state, validated general bundles, per-target loading, and operator-visible reversal. Atomic activation of a coordinated policy set across disconnected targets, automatic compatibility decisions, KPI-triggered rollback, and complete TI-MARL serving remain outside the implemented guarantee. These are not peripheral deployment details: they determine whether the policy combination observed at runtime is the combination that was validated.

RQ4 is therefore answered by a concrete lifecycle and by the boundaries it exposes. Deployability is demonstrated through preserved meaning, target compatibility, observable activation, and recoverable state. Continued coordinated operation requires further evidence when several policies and intermittent targets must change together.

\section{Interactions among the Four Requirements}
\label{sec:discussion-interactions}
\label{sec:discussion-tradeoffs}

The four requirements affect one another, often in ways that a single reward or benchmark conceals.

\paragraph{Scalability and control quality.}
Parameter sharing and aggregation reduce model growth, but aggressive aggregation can remove distinctions required for local service or phase-specific limits. More expressive typed interfaces preserve those distinctions at the cost of additional tokens, compilation, and trace data. Scale must therefore be reported together with the control information retained.

\paragraph{Safety and performance.}
The local projector can reject a nominally profitable action because it violates service or electrical limits. That reduction in reward is not a loss of controller quality under the physical problem. Frequent projection, however, indicates that the actor has not internalised the feasible region. Raw and final bundles are needed to distinguish valid conservatism from weak learning.

\paragraph{Robustness and specialisation.}
A policy tuned to one community, season, or stable asset set may outperform a more transferable policy in nominal operation. Typed representations, health-aware compilation, historical data, and broader scenario coverage can improve transfer while increasing training and validation cost. The preferred balance depends on how often the site changes and how expensive retraining or a failed decision is.

\paragraph{Deployability and model complexity.}
A controller can pass simulation gates and still exceed a target's memory or latency budget. A model can fit each target separately while its preprocessing, compiler, communication, or update protocol remains incompatible. Resource and compatibility gates belong to promotion because they influence which controller can be used, not merely how it is installed.

\paragraph{Reconfiguration and assurance drift.}
An interface change can invalidate a trained action head, an observation alias, a safety bound, a fallback dependency, or a previously validated policy combination. The typed interface and lifecycle meet at this point. The controller must expose the current contract, and the operational system must decide whether the resulting composition remains admissible.

\section{Scientific Contributions, Generality, and Responsible Use}
\label{sec:discussion-contributions}

The central scientific contribution is TI-MARL's executable connection between a versioned control contract and multi-agent learning. Its novelty does not lie in PPO, attention, parameter sharing, health rules, or projection separately. The method compiles an entity-bound interface; evaluates it through one shared typed actor; associates decisions with current groups and physical meanings; applies health, validity, bounds, and local feasibility through the same semantics; and carries stable identities and compatibility evidence across topology change. TI-PPO and TI-MAPPO isolate the effect of local and central-set value estimation while retaining that common actor and runtime path.

This principal contribution rests on six supporting outcomes: the evidence-based formulation of the integrated real-world MARL problem; the EnergAIze research architecture; the audited simulator and annual scenario suite; the progression of control and learning solutions; the experiment and inference lifecycle; and the demonstrator evidence. They should not be presented as seven unrelated novelties. Together they explain why the algorithm has the interface it has and how its claims can be tested and carried towards use.

The empirical conclusions remain energy-domain conclusions. A typed charger action does not become a robotic manipulation action by renaming it. The transferable part is the problem structure: agents can gain or lose known capabilities; observations and actions belong to identifiable entities; availability changes validity and bounds; local decisions interact through shared resources; and policies must retain their meaning across training and runtime. Application to robotics, industrial equipment, traffic control, or distributed edge services requires new schemas, physical constraints, baselines, and evidence.

Responsible community control also requires participant-level interpretation. Lower aggregate cost can coexist with worse outcomes for a minority, and average EV service can hide one missed departure. The scorecard therefore includes benefit coverage, service, constraints, and intervention alongside energy and cost. Local inference reduces some data transfer but does not remove privacy risks from logs, forecasts, identities, or model updates. Cybersecurity, certification, regulation, and long-term organisational governance constrain deployment but are not claimed as primary contributions of this thesis.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:discussion-validity}

\paragraph{Construct validity.}
Dynamic population, safety, robustness, scalability, and real-world operation have several meanings. The thesis separates session changes from member admission, local projection from shared physical safety, nominal noise from declared faults, population size from resource scale, and real-data simulation from physical control. Some ambiguity remains when source projects use broader terminology.

\paragraph{Internal validity.}
RL comparisons depend on implementation, reward, information access, tuning effort, checkpoint selection, seeds, and stochastic environments. Common manifests, paired windows, hard promotion gates, strong baselines, ablations, and independent seeds reduce this risk. They cannot compensate for an unequal information or tuning budget, which must remain visible in each comparison.

\paragraph{External validity.}
The communities, assets, tariffs, weather, participants, devices, and event distributions bound the results. Core and Premium scenarios broaden controlled coverage, while OPEVA and DemFlex expose conditions absent from nominal simulation. Neither large simulation nor one demonstrator establishes universal performance.

\paragraph{Physical and causal validity.}
The simulator and local projector depend on the correctness of their electrical, asset, service, and settlement models. Field outcomes can be confounded by occupancy, weather, price, maintenance, participant behaviour, platform changes, and manual intervention. Matched baselines and replay improve interpretation but do not create randomised field evidence.

\paragraph{Implementation and attribution.}
The work builds on CityLearn, established algorithms, open-source packages, student work, partner platforms, and collaborative European projects. Releases, repository history, contribution statements, data ownership, and demonstrator records are required to distinguish original design, adaptation, implementation, integration, data provision, and experiment execution.

\section{Implications and Open Research Problems}
\label{sec:discussion-summary}
\label{sec:discussion-open}

The results point to a smaller set of open problems than the broad real-world MARL label suggests. Unknown asset families still require admission semantics, policy selection, and safety configuration. Shared hard constraints need allocation mechanisms whose effect on fairness and service can be evaluated. Assurance rules must detect when their own observations or physical models are no longer trustworthy. Continual and offline improvement must preserve promotion evidence and previously validated behaviour. Coordinated updates over intermittent networks need explicit mixed-version and local recovery semantics. Longer replicated deployments are required to study seasonal drift, maintenance, interventions, and benefit distribution.

The thesis therefore answers the four research questions at different evidence levels. RQ1 receives a concrete typed-interface formulation and verified implementation. RQ2 receives mechanisms and an explicit multi-dimensional evaluation envelope. RQ3 receives layered local feasibility, health-aware operation, disturbance protocols, and a clear boundary around shared safety. RQ4 receives an implemented experiment-to-inference lifecycle with honest limits around coordinated rollout and TI-MARL serving. The quantitative strength of each answer remains tied to the final evidence reported in its contribution chapter.

The main conclusion is that the difficult part of real-world MARL lies in preserving meaning across layers. The physical problem, simulator, policy interface, feasibility logic, experiment, package, target, and applied action cannot be evaluated as unrelated objects. EnergAIze provides the system context for those relations, and TI-MARL makes the changing local control contract an explicit part of learning. Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusions} condenses this argument and states what the thesis ultimately establishes.

\chapter{TI-MARL: Typed-Interface Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Reconfigurable Cyber-Physical Systems}
\label{chap:ti-marl}
\markboth{TI-MARL}{TI-MARL}

Chapter~\ref{chap:control-benchmark} followed the controller research from transparent rules and optimisation to single-agent RL, conventional MARL, entity-structured policies, knowledge reuse, and hierarchical coordination. That progression solved real parts of the energy-control problem, but its learning interfaces remained partly fixed. A controller could learn to schedule the assets it had been given; changing the number of agents, the instances owned by one agent, the meaning of an observation, or the set of safe actions could still invalidate the model or the software around it.

The distinction matters in an operational community. Adding a second charger does not merely append another number to the state. It introduces a physical module, an independently occupied connection, a new EV session, another action group, and new competition for the same total and per-phase electrical limits. A stale EV state of charge does not have the same consequence as a missing price forecast. The first can make V2G unsafe; the second can leave local operation available under poorer economic information. When a member joins or leaves, the population changes without erasing the temporal history of the members that remain. Fixed vectors, masks, and manually rebuilt heads can represent bounded versions of these cases, but they do not by themselves preserve what each value and action means.

\Gls{timarl} was developed to make that changing interface an explicit part of the control problem. The implemented method begins with one technology-neutral typed contract for each registered member. A deterministic compiler binds that contract to the entities, runtime values, topology events, and health evidence supplied by the simulator. A parameter-shared local actor then processes the variable set of observations and active action groups, while an analytic projector applies the local electrical and service constraints derived from the same interface. TI-PPO uses a local typed critic; TI-MAPPO replaces it with a permutation-equivariant central set critic during training. Both execute through decentralised actors.

The running example is a building with two EV chargers, a stationary battery, and a deferrable service. One charger may be V2G-capable while the other is charge-only. Their EV sessions have different arrival states, targets, and departure times, yet both chargers share the building connection and can lie on different phases. This example exposes the four requirements used throughout the thesis. Dynamic instances and stable semantic types address heterogeneous control; shared parameters and variable-cardinality encoders address scalability; health closure and local projection address safety and robustness; versioned interfaces, compatibility signatures, checkpoints, and execution traces establish the algorithm-side basis for deployability.

This chapter explains the method as it exists in the Algorithms repository rather than as a list of possible extensions. It identifies the contribution precisely, develops the compiler--actor--projector--learner path, records the engineering decisions needed to train it, and fixes the experimental protocol. Quantitative control results remain provisional until the confirmation campaign is frozen. Verified software and runtime evidence is reported separately from those pending comparisons.

\section{Scientific Positioning and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:timarl-positioning}

\subsection{Closest Approaches and the Residual Problem}
\label{sec:timarl-closest-work}

Several neighbouring research lines already solve parts of the problem. Parameterised-action methods combine a discrete action type with continuous parameters \parencite{xiong2018pdqn,li2022hyar}. Variable-action policies can process action candidates whose cardinality, order, or semantic content changes \parencite{jain2020newactions,sinii2024headless}. Modular and cross-embodiment policies reuse one controller across different sensors, actuators, and morphologies \parencite{huang2020smp,luketina2025compositional,doshi2025crossformer}. Heterogeneous and task-open MARL address different action semantics, changing tasks, and dynamic populations \parencite{yu2024uas,anil2025mohito}. Sensor-dropout training improves robustness to missing modalities \parencite{liu2017sensordropout}, while joint-action shields prevent unsafe combinations of actions \parencite{elsayedaly2021shielding}.

Variable action sets, parameterised actions, heterogeneous agents, changing populations, graph encoders, actor--critic optimisation, and safety monitors are established classes of mechanism. TI-MARL contributes the relationship among them. The same runtime contract should identify the active entities, bind observations to their sources, instantiate the action groups and ports that currently exist, propagate health and dependency changes into port validity and bounds, preserve local information boundaries, and remain meaningful to the policy, critic, safety logic, deployment validator, and explanation trace.

This position is especially important for assets that enter and leave. A padded universal vector can reserve ten charger slots and mask the unused positions. Such a representation is often sufficient when the maximum topology and all action semantics are known in advance. It becomes less natural when a newly admitted module brings a coherent observation--action contract, when several instances of the same type must be distinguished, or when a failed observation changes the safe domain of only the dependent actions. TI-MARL tests whether an explicit entity-bound typed interface provides a useful inductive and operational structure in those cases.

The method builds on the progression established in \cref{chap:control-benchmark}. \Cref{tab:timarl-progression} identifies the problem solved by each family and the assumption that TI-MARL relaxes.

The scientific claim concerns decision problems in which the interface itself is a time-varying structured object. The novelty is not PPO, attention, parameter sharing, health rules, or action projection in isolation. It lies in their executable connection: a versioned per-agent contract is compiled into entity-bound observations and action groups; the shared actor consumes that compiled object; validity, dynamic bounds, health closure, and local feasibility use the same semantics; and stable identities carry transitions, checkpoints, compatibility decisions, and traces across topology change. The experiments in \cref{sec:timarl-methodology,sec:timarl-results} test whether this connection offers value beyond masked, entity-structured, and conventional dynamic-population baselines.

\subsection{Typed-Interface Decision Processes}
\label{sec:timarl-decision-processes}

The one-agent reduction isolates interface reconfiguration from cross-agent learning. A \gls{timdp} is defined by

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{TI}} =
\left\langle
\mathcal{S},\Sigma,\mathcal{H},\mathcal{C},\Gamma^{\mathrm{loc}},P,P_{\Sigma},R,\gamma
\right\rangle,
\label{eq:timdp-tuple}
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal{S}$ is the physical and operational state space, $\Sigma_t$ is the current versioned capability schema, $\mathcal{H}_t$ is the runtime health state, $\mathcal{C}$ is the Typed Interface Compiler, and $\Gamma^{\mathrm{loc}}_t$ is the local typed relation and constraint graph. $P_{\Sigma}$ represents interface events such as module addition, module removal, sensor degradation, or recovery. Learning in this one-agent model is denoted \gls{tirl}. It supports controlled evaluation of multiple chargers, variable action groups, health-aware contraction, and local bundle feasibility without introducing multi-agent credit assignment.

A \gls{timg} adds an active agent population, local information boundaries, coupled outcomes, and shared resources:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{TI}} =
\left\langle
\mathcal{S},\mathcal{N},\Sigma,\mathcal{H},\mathcal{C},\Gamma,P,P_{\Sigma},\{R_i\}_{i\in\mathcal{N}},\gamma
\right\rangle.
\label{eq:timg-tuple}
\end{equation}

At time $t$, $\mathcal{N}_t\subseteq\mathcal{N}$ denotes the active population. Each agent $i$ has a schema $\Sigma_{i,t}$ and health state $\mathcal{H}_{i,t}$. The physical state is augmented with the active population and interface state:

\begin{equation}
\tilde{s}_t =
\left(
 s_t,
 \mathcal{N}_t,
 \{\Sigma_{i,t},\mathcal{H}_{i,t}\}_{i\in\mathcal{N}_t},
 \Gamma_t
\right).
\label{eq:timg-augmented-state}
\end{equation}

The compiler produces the policy-facing interface of agent $i$:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{I}_{i,t}
=
\mathcal{C}
\left(
\Sigma_{i,t},
\mathcal{H}_{i,t},
\operatorname{view}_i(\tilde{s}_t)
\right)
=
\left(
\mathcal{V}_{i,t},
\mathcal{E}_{i,t},
\mathcal{G}_{i,t}^{a},
\mathcal{P}_{i,t},
\mathcal{B}_{i,t}
\right),
\label{eq:interface-compiler}
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal{V}_{i,t}$ and $\mathcal{E}_{i,t}$ are the typed interface nodes and relations, $\mathcal{G}_{i,t}^{a}$ is the variable set of active action-group instances, $\mathcal{P}_{i,t}$ is the set of valid action-port instances, and $\mathcal{B}_{i,t}$ contains the current parameter domains and local hard constraints. The view function enforces the information boundary: local actors receive their local interface and explicitly authorised aggregate context, not the private observations of other agents.

The policy produces one decision for each active action-group instance:

\begin{equation}
a_{i,t}
=
\left\{
 a_{i,g,t}
\right\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}_{i,t}^{a}},
\qquad
 a_{i,g,t}
=
\left(
 p_{i,g,t},\eta_{i,g,t}
\right),
\label{eq:local-action-bundle}
\end{equation}

where $p_{i,g,t}$ is a selected valid port and $\eta_{i,g,t}$ is its parameter vector. The local action is a variable structured bundle whose contents follow the active interface. A constraint-aware construction $\Phi_i$ maps raw decoded group decisions into a locally feasible bundle:

\begin{equation}
\bar{a}_{i,t}
=
\Phi_i
\left(
 a_{i,t},
 \Gamma^{\mathrm{loc}}_{i,t}
\right),
\qquad
\bar{a}_{i,t}\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{i,t}.
\label{eq:local-feasible-bundle}
\end{equation}

The multi-agent joint action is

\begin{equation}
\bar{\mathbf{a}}_t
=
\left(
\bar{a}_{i,t}
\right)_{i\in\mathcal{N}_t}.
\label{eq:joint-action}
\end{equation}

In the implemented v1 path, $\bar{\mathbf{a}}_t$ is encoded into simulator commands and the simulator remains authoritative for the applied action and residual physical violation. The analytic projector acts within each member and therefore enforces declared local total and per-phase headroom; it does not redistribute power between members. A future shared corrector could be expressed as $\mathcal{S}_{\Gamma}(\bar{\mathbf{a}}_t,\Gamma_t)$, but no result in this chapter relies on one. The physical transition is

\begin{equation}
s_{t+1}
\sim
P
\left(
\cdot\mid s_t,\bar{\mathbf{a}}_t
\right).
\label{eq:timg-transition}
\end{equation}

Local and community outcomes are coupled through this joint transition. A representative reward decomposition is

\begin{equation}
r_{i,t}
=
r^{\mathrm{loc}}_{i,t}
+
\alpha_i r^{\mathrm{com}}_t
-
\beta_i c^{\mathrm{loc}}_{i,t}
-
\eta_i c^{\mathrm{shared}}_t,
\label{eq:timarl-reward}
\end{equation}

where local terms capture cost, service, comfort, or degradation; the community term captures peak, ramping, renewable utilisation, or a shared demand-response objective; and the constraint terms distinguish local and shared violations or interventions. When $|\mathcal{N}_t|=1$ and $\Gamma_t$ contains only local relations, the model reduces to TI-RL.

The formal objects are domain-independent. In energy, a module can be a charger or battery, an entity can be an EV session, a community observation can be transformer loading, and a port can be \textsc{charge-ev}$(p)$. In robotics, the same structure can represent a sensor module, a manipulated object, workspace occupancy, and a parameterised grasp action. The thesis evaluates the method in energy systems while preserving this broader interpretation.

The reduction also clarifies the experimental sequence. TI-PPO tests whether the typed local interface and its reconfiguration can be learned without central training. TI-MAPPO keeps the same actor and execution path but uses the active population during value estimation. Differences between the two can therefore be attributed to centralised training rather than to a different deployed controller.

\section{Entity-Bound Runtime Typed Interfaces}
\label{sec:runtime-interface-model}

\subsection{Schemas, Entities, and Dynamic Module Instances}
\label{sec:timarl-entities}

A capability schema describes the potential interface of an agent. It identifies the agent role, module types, observation templates, action-group templates, constraints, compatibility requirements, and fallback semantics. Runtime state identifies which module instances and controlled entities are active now. Separating the two prevents fast-changing values such as SoC or connection state from being treated as schema revisions.

A building can contain several instances of the same module type. In the running example, agent $A$ contains two charger instances, one stationary battery, one deferrable-service module, and one main meter:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}_{A,t}
=
\{m^{\mathrm{meter}}_1,
  m^{\mathrm{charger}}_1,
  m^{\mathrm{charger}}_2,
  m^{\mathrm{battery}}_1,
  m^{\mathrm{def}}_1\}.
\label{eq:running-modules}
\end{equation}

Each charger can be related to a currently connected EV session. The observation

\begin{quote}\small
\texttt{semantic type: EV\_SOC}\\
\texttt{entity: ev\_session\_17}\\
\texttt{source module: charger\_1}
\end{quote}

cannot be confused with the SoC of the vehicle connected to \texttt{charger\_2}. The corresponding action group is similarly bound:

\begin{quote}\small
\texttt{group type: EV\_CHARGER\_MODE}\\
\texttt{owner module: charger\_1}\\
\texttt{target entity: ev\_session\_17}
\end{quote}

The compiler represents these relationships as typed edges such as \texttt{owns}, \texttt{connected\_to}, \texttt{observes}, \texttt{controls}, and \texttt{requires}. Entity binding is encoded in the interface semantics and does not depend on position in a flat vector.

When \texttt{charger\_3} is installed, a module-addition event creates a new module node, its observation parts, one EV-charger action-group instance, valid port instances, and the relevant constraint edges. The neural network is not extended with a new permanent head. The same group and port decoder used for the existing chargers is applied to the new instance. Removing the charger removes that subgraph. A semantically unknown module is admitted only when a compatible parent type and decoder are available; otherwise its learned control path remains inactive and fallback semantics apply.

The implementation deliberately separates four identifiers that flat action layouts often conflate. A member owns the local controller boundary; a charger identifies installed infrastructure; an EV identifies a vehicle; and a session records the temporary occupancy of one charger by one EV. A domestic charger can retain the same vehicle over many sessions, whereas one office charger can serve several vehicles in one day. The action group belongs to the physical charger, while service observations belong to the currently bound session. Session replacement therefore rebinds runtime values without rebuilding an unchanged charger structure.

\subsection{Typed Observation Parts and Community Context}
\label{sec:timarl-observations}

An observation part is a typed object with an identity and declared semantics. A representative definition is

\begin{equation}
o_k
=
\left(
\tau_k,
\mathrm{id}_k,
\mathrm{entity}_k,
\mathrm{source}_k,
 x_k,
 u_k,
 q_k,
 h_k,
 t_k,
 s_k
\right),
\label{eq:observation-part}
\end{equation}

where $\tau_k$ is the semantic type, $x_k$ the value, $u_k$ the unit, $q_k$ quality or uncertainty metadata, $h_k$ the health state, $t_k$ the timestamp, and $s_k$ the scope. The scope can be self, local, neighbourhood, community, or global. The source can be a physical sensor, derived signal, forecast, external platform, or coordinator message.

Community information is represented through the same object model. A building may receive numerical aggregates such as community net demand, transformer loading, remaining headroom, photovoltaic surplus, a price multiplier, or a demand-response target. These values do not reveal the raw private state of another building. Their provenance and health remain explicit:

\begin{quote}\small
\texttt{semantic type: COMMUNITY\_TRANSFORMER\_LOADING}\\
\texttt{scope: community; source: platform\_service}\\
\texttt{unit: percent; health: HEALTHY}
\end{quote}

TI-MARL does not require flexibility to be represented through an explicit negotiation object. In the energy instantiation, an EV connected from 08:00 until 18:00 with a current SoC, target SoC, maximum charging power, and departure requirement already exposes the information from which a policy can learn temporal flexibility. Useful derived quantities such as time to departure, remaining required energy, or required average charging power can be supplied as typed observation parts when they improve learning or interpretability. Their use is an experimental design choice and introduces no separate offer phase or community allocator.

\subsection{Dynamic Action Groups and Typed Parameterised Ports}
\label{sec:timarl-action-ports}

The output interface is organised around action-group instances. Each controllable module creates one or more groups whose ports are mutually exclusive within the group but can be combined with ports from other groups. For example,

\begin{align}
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{charger}_1,t}
&=\{\textsc{idle-ev},\textsc{charge-ev}(p),\textsc{discharge-ev}(p)\},\\
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{battery}_1,t}
&=\{\textsc{idle-battery},\textsc{charge-battery}(p),\textsc{discharge-battery}(p)\},\\
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{def}_1,t}
&=\{\textsc{idle},\textsc{start}\}.
\end{align}

An action port is a typed callable capability:

\begin{equation}
p
=
\left(
\tau_p,
\mathrm{id}_p,
\mathrm{group}_p,
\mathrm{owner}_p,
\Theta_p,
U_p,
B_{p,t},
V_{p,t},
D_p,
K_p
\right),
\label{eq:action-port}
\end{equation}

where $\Theta_p$ is the parameter schema, $U_p$ the units, $B_{p,t}$ the current parameter domain, $V_{p,t}$ the validity state, $D_p$ the observation and capability dependencies, and $K_p$ the resource effects used by local constraints, critics, and optional safety checks.

A new charger creates another instance of the charger action group. It does not create another set of network parameters. The implemented actor emits a categorical mode and a Beta-distributed fraction in $[0,1]$ for every active group. The fraction is relative to the currently compiled physical upper bound and is converted to a signed simulator command only once by the codec. This convention avoids applying a health-dependent or topology-dependent bound twice. A deferrable start is indivisible: after \textsc{start} is selected, its typed cycle profile determines the subsequent demand rather than a continuous power fraction.

Discharge EV & charger 2 / session 28 & power [kW] & absent & charger 2 has no V2G capability \\
Discharge battery & battery 1 & power [kW] & valid, $[0,3.0]$ & battery SoC and reserve; reduces house import \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

For example, suppose \texttt{charger\_1} is rated at 7.4~kW but the current connection and EV state reduce its compiled charging bound to 4.0~kW. A sampled fraction of 0.75 requests 3.0~kW. The network output remains in the same numerical domain when the available bound later changes; the interface gives the output its current physical meaning. The trace stores the selected mode, fraction, compiled bound, requested command, projected command, and applied simulator value separately.

\subsection{Health-Aware Causal Compilation}
\label{sec:timarl-health}

Health is part of the interface semantics and is also available to the neural network. The implemented states are \textsc{healthy}, \textsc{degraded}, \textsc{stale}, \textsc{missing}, \textsc{failed}, and \textsc{unknown}. Fault evidence remains a separate record. A simulator event such as \texttt{dropout} or \texttt{stuck} is not mapped to one universal health label; the compiler considers event duration, freshness, criticality, declared availability, channel semantics, and recovery hysteresis.

A charger failure disables only the associated charger group and its ports. Other modules controlled by the member remain active. A stale EV SoC can preserve charging while blocking V2G because the discharge reserve can no longer be established. Failure of the main house meter has a wider consequence: the complete member is isolated when aggregate import or phase headroom cannot be verified. Loss of a community signal removes that coordination input but leaves the local controller available. During a grid outage, charging, V2G, and a new deferrable start are blocked; safe stationary-storage discharge may remain available when its local dependencies still hold.

The compiler computes the transitive effect of a health event through a dependency closure:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{H}^{\star}_{i,t}
=
\operatorname{Closure}
\left(
\mathcal{H}_{i,t},D_{i,t}
\right),
\label{eq:health-closure}
\end{equation}

which identifies affected observations, ports, groups, bounds, local constraints, and operational modes. For the EV SoC example, the closure can be summarised as

\begin{align}
\textsc{healthy} &\Rightarrow \textsc{discharge-ev}\in[0,P^{\mathrm{v2g}}_{\max,t}],\\
\textsc{stale} &\Rightarrow \textsc{discharge-ev}\notin\mathcal{P}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{valid}},\\
\textsc{failed} &\Rightarrow \textsc{discharge-ev}\notin\mathcal{P}_{i,t}^{\mathrm{valid}}.
\end{align}

This behaviour differs from replacing a missing value with zero and asking the policy to infer the safety consequence. The dependency rule changes the decision interface itself. Recovery is explicit: a group or port returns only after its declared fresh-data and hysteresis conditions hold. The closure log records the subject, state, rule, consequence, and duration used in that decision.

The compiler rebuilds structural plans only when topology changes. Values, health evidence, session identity, validity, and bounds are refreshed at every step without treating routine runtime churn as a new neural architecture. This separation is important in the 15-second stress case, where value and session updates occur far more frequently than asset installation or removal.

\section{Local Typed-Interface Policies}
\label{sec:tip-and-learning}

\subsection{Local Information Boundaries and Parameter Sharing}
\label{sec:timarl-local-info}

Each agent executes a logically local \gls{tip}. A building actor receives its compiled interface, optional memory, and authorised aggregate community observations. It does not receive the raw loads, EV states, or private service requirements of other buildings. This boundary is identical during evaluation and deployment; privileged information used by a central critic during training is not silently passed to the actor.

The current implementation shares one actor across all compatible members:

\begin{equation}
\pi_i
=
\pi_{\theta}
\left(
\mathcal{I}_{i,t},m_{i,t}
\right),
\label{eq:role-sharing}
\end{equation}

Parameter sharing does not imply shared observations. House $A$ and House $B$ execute the same weights on different local snapshots, and member role remains an encoded feature. The compiler rejects unknown semantic or action-group types rather than silently allocating a random representation. This keeps zero-shot admission limited to new instances and compositions of known contracts.

This structure supports population changes without a permanent actor slot per agent. A new building instantiates the appropriate local policy family after schema compatibility has been checked. Removing a building removes its actor instance from the active population; the shared parameter set and the actors of the remaining agents are unchanged.

\subsection{Hierarchical Typed Encoding and Local Latent State}
\label{sec:timarl-local-encoder}

The compiled local interface is encoded as variable-cardinality typed sets. The hierarchy follows the simulator's measurement structure: observation parts are grouped into channels, channels into sensors, and sensors into the local member representation. Structural relations remain available when an action group asks for its relevant observations. This is narrower than a general graph neural network and matches the implementation used in the experiments.

Each observation token combines its normalised value with quality and validity indicators, health, semantic type, sensor type, channel type, unit, scope, declared use, a deterministic semantic fingerprint, and temporal-horizon features. The first mapping is

\begin{align}
z^{o}_{k}
&=
E^{o}_{\theta}
\left(
x_k,q_k,h_k,e_{\tau_k},e_{\mathrm{sensor}},e_{\mathrm{channel}},
e_{u_k},e_{s_k},e_{\mathrm{use}},f^{\mathrm{id}}_k,f^{\mathrm{time}}_k
\right).
\end{align}

Permutation-equivariant relational layers aggregate observation tokens within their channels and sensors, followed by learned pooling over the local sensor set:

\begin{equation}
z_{i,t}
=
F_{\theta}
\left(
\left\{\left\{\{z^o_k\}_{k\in c}\right\}_{c\in s}\right\}_{s\in i}
\right).
\label{eq:tip-encoder}
\end{equation}

The common latent $z_{i,t}$ summarises the complete local decision context. Concrete asset identifiers are not learned as permanent embeddings. Instance distinction is carried by the compiled structural binding and deterministic observation identity, while semantic families determine reusable parameters. Reordering observations, chargers, or members therefore does not change the intended meaning.

Each active action group starts from its type embedding and the local latent. In the action-conditioned variant, the group also queries observation tokens related to its owner, target entity, and dependencies. The two chargers can use the same mode and fraction heads while attending to different sessions. Attention across the active group set then exposes local competition before decoding. Adding a charger adds one group evaluation; it does not change the actor or critic parameter count.

During replay and behaviour-cloning updates, immutable type indices and set membership are prepared once and reused across optimisation epochs. Runtime values and health are never cached as static input. Packed evaluation removes repeated per-agent device transfers while preserving the same set semantics. These changes affect throughput, not the mathematical information surface.

\subsection{Dynamic Grouped Action Decoding}
\label{sec:timarl-action-decoder}

The actor does not contain a permanent neural head for each asset instance. A group-type head is applied to every active group of that type. For group $g$ and candidate mode $m$,

\begin{equation}
\ell_{i,g,m,t}
=
f^{\mathrm{select}}_{\theta}
\left(
 z_{i,t},z_{i,g,t},e_{\tau_g}
\right),
\label{eq:port-logit}
\end{equation}

and the categorical mode is selected after invalid modes have been masked:

\begin{equation}
\Pr
\left(
 m_{i,g,t}=m
\right)
=
\operatorname{Softmax}_{m\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{valid}}_{i,g,t}}
\left(
\ell_{i,g,m,t}
\right).
\label{eq:port-selection}
\end{equation}

A second head generates positive Beta parameters. Sampling $u_{i,g,t}\sim\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha_{i,g,t},\beta_{i,g,t})$ gives the bounded fraction

\begin{equation}
\eta_{i,g,t}
=
u_{i,g,t},
\qquad
(\alpha_{i,g,t},\beta_{i,g,t})
=
d_{\tau_g}(z_{i,g,t}).
\label{eq:port-parameters}
\end{equation}

The log probability used by PPO is the sum of the categorical and Beta terms. At training time the two parts are sampled. Deterministic replay can use categorical argmax or an \texttt{expected\_signed} rule that combines charge and discharge probabilities, the Beta mean, a configurable gain, a deadband, and a non-idle logit margin. These controls were added because an apparently small stochastic preference can become an implausibly persistent deterministic charge or discharge command after export.

All groups are decoded as one bundle from the same local context. A representative raw bundle is

\begin{equation}
a^{\mathrm{raw}}_{A,t}
=
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
\texttt{charger\_1}:\textsc{charge-ev}(4.0\ \mathrm{kW}),\\
\texttt{charger\_2}:\textsc{charge-ev}(3.5\ \mathrm{kW}),\\
\texttt{battery\_1}:\textsc{discharge-battery}(1.0\ \mathrm{kW}),\\
\texttt{deferrable\_1}:\textsc{start}
\end{array}
\right\}.
\label{eq:running-raw-bundle}
\end{equation}

The bundle-level representation allows the actor to learn priorities among the two EV sessions, battery use, a pending service, and community conditions. It does not by itself guarantee that the combined power remains within local electrical limits or that an urgent service remains feasible. Those requirements are handled by the final local construction.

\subsection{Constraint-Aware Local Bundle Construction}
\label{sec:timarl-local-feasibility}

Local hard constraints should not depend exclusively on a negative reward. An actor can learn to reduce violations, but safety-critical electrical and service limits require explicit semantics. TI-MARL keeps the learned preference in the raw bundle and applies a constraint-aware parameterisation to obtain the final local bundle.

Let $a^{\mathrm{raw}}_{i,t}$ denote the decoded bundle and $\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{i,t}$ the feasible set induced by the local typed graph. The construction is

\begin{equation}
\bar{a}_{i,t}
=
\Phi_i
\left(
 a^{\mathrm{raw}}_{i,t},
 \mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{i,t}
\right),
\qquad
\bar{a}_{i,t}\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{i,t}.
\label{eq:local-bundle-construction}
\end{equation}

The v1 mapping is an analytic joint projector. It first rejects a removed or invalid group, applies a forced typed fallback when one exists, clips the learned fraction to $[0,1]$, and respects the minimum operating power declared by the selected port. It then reserves mandatory deferrable starts and the EV charging power needed to remain within the configured service schedule. Remaining charge and discharge requests are jointly scaled against total and phase-specific import or export headroom. A command that falls below its typed operating floor after scaling returns to \textsc{idle}.

For the running example, one local constraint is

\begin{equation}
P^{\mathrm{ch1}}_t
+
P^{\mathrm{ch2}}_t
+
P^{\mathrm{def}}_t
-
P^{\mathrm{bess,dis}}_t
\leq
P^{\max}_{A,t}.
\label{eq:running-local-constraint}
\end{equation}

This total limit is evaluated together with phase incidence. If both chargers lie on L1, unused headroom on L3 cannot make their combination feasible. A three-phase service contributes to all three phase rows according to its declared incidence. The projector can retain a configurable headroom reserve and a completion margin for deferrables. It does not compare one member with another or optimise the community objective.

Two diagnostics distinguish a useful feasibility mechanism from one that hides poor policy learning:

\begin{align}
I^{\mathrm{loc}}_t
&=\mathbb{1}\left[a^{\mathrm{raw}}_{i,t}\notin\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{i,t}\right],\\
D^{\mathrm{loc}}_t
&=\left\|\bar{a}_{i,t}-a^{\mathrm{raw}}_{i,t}\right\|.
\end{align}

Frequent or large adjustments indicate reliance on the projector and weak learning of coherent bundles. Requested, projected, and applied actions remain separate in the transition record, so a physically safe trajectory cannot conceal an infeasible raw policy. The optional shield-aware update removes an intervened group from the PPO policy loss and can distil the final feasible decision into that group; unrelated groups remain eligible for learning.

\section{Multi-Agent Learning and Community Coordination}
\label{sec:timarl-multiagent-learning}

\subsection{Joint Training and Decentralised Execution}
\label{sec:timarl-joint-training}

TI-MARL does not require a community controller agent. Each building acts independently from its own compiled interface and authorised community observations. The method becomes multi-agent because the local bundles form one joint action, the physical transition depends on that joint action, local actions compete for shared resources, and rewards include local and community consequences.

At one training step,

\begin{equation}
\bar{\mathbf{a}}_t
=
\left(
\pi_1(\mathcal{I}_{1,t}),
\ldots,
\pi_n(\mathcal{I}_{n,t})
\right)
\end{equation}

is applied to the shared environment. The transition produces the next local interfaces, local rewards, and common community outcomes. A low community headroom followed by simultaneous charging from two buildings can increase peak and ramping penalties or create a residual simulator violation. Over repeated interaction, the agents learn how local state and authorised aggregate signals relate to the behaviour of the other agents.

Joint training means that actor updates are derived from the same joint transition. It does not mean that actors share private runtime inputs or that one global actor replaces them. Stable member identities govern the rollout when population changes. A joining member has no predecessor in Generalised Advantage Estimation; a departing member receives a terminal boundary; members that remain can bootstrap across the topology event. The rollout is not cleared and the networks are not resized.

\subsection{Local and Central Set Critics}
\label{sec:timarl-central-critic}

A conventional central critic often concatenates all agent observations. That representation is tied to a fixed order and population. TI-PPO avoids centralisation and estimates $V_i$ from the local typed snapshot. TI-MAPPO retains the same actor but estimates values from the variable active set.

The implemented central critic encodes each member with the same typed snapshot encoder, mean-pools the active local latents into a community summary, and evaluates each stable member identity from its local and community representations:

\begin{equation}
V_{\phi,i,t}
=
g_{\phi}
\left(
z_{i,t},
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{N}_t|}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_t}z_{j,t}
\right).
\label{eq:graph-critic}
\end{equation}

The mapping is permutation equivariant: reordering members reorders their values but does not alter their association. Cardinality can change without reconstructing a joint input layer. Mean pooling is intentionally modest. It captures community context and supports the current single-REC experiments, but it does not distinguish several feeder neighbourhoods with identical aggregate summaries. A typed resource-graph critic is a reasonable extension for that case and is not claimed as part of v1.

\subsection{TI-PPO and TI-MAPPO}
\label{sec:timarl-backbones}

TI-MARL defines the interface, actor structure, local bundle semantics, and population handling. The current learner supports two controlled PPO variants.

\paragraph{TI-PPO.}
The shared actor and a parameter-shared local critic are updated from local member trajectories. This is the typed one-agent learner applied independently across the active population. It isolates the effect of the interface and actor from centralised value estimation.

\paragraph{TI-MAPPO.}
The actor is paired with the central set critic in \cref{eq:graph-critic}. The critic may use the active joint training context; the actor retains its local information boundary. A checkpoint pins the backbone and critic kind, so a TI-PPO state cannot be loaded into TI-MAPPO by accident.

For a transition generated by old parameters $\theta_{\mathrm{old}}$, the hybrid group probability ratio is

\begin{equation}
r_{i,g,t}(\theta)
=
\exp\left[
\log\pi_{\theta}(m_{i,g,t},\eta_{i,g,t}\mid\mathcal{I}_{i,t})
-
\log\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(m_{i,g,t},\eta_{i,g,t}\mid\mathcal{I}_{i,t})
\right].
\label{eq:timarl-ratio}
\end{equation}

With joint-agent credit, the clipped PPO objective uses the sum of group log probabilities for that member. With typed-group credit, the ratio and advantage remain attached to group $g$:

\begin{equation}
L^{\mathrm{group}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{i,g,t}
\left[
\min\left(
r_{i,g,t}(\theta)\hat{A}_{i,g,t},
\operatorname{clip}(r_{i,g,t}(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)
\hat{A}_{i,g,t}
\right)
\right].
\label{eq:timarl-group-ppo}
\end{equation}

The group critic supplies a matching baseline. EV service and charger-constraint consequences are routed to the responsible charger family; stationary-storage consequences reach the storage group; deferrable completion reaches its start group. Economic settlement and genuinely shared grid effects remain common. This avoids teaching the second charger that it caused a service failure belonging to the first, while preserving community coupling.

TI-MATD3 was considered because Chapter~\ref{chap:control-benchmark} found value in deterministic twin-critic methods. It would require a principled relaxation of the categorical mode and a variable-population twin critic. That implementation is not part of the present contribution and is retained as future comparative work rather than a method evaluated here.

\subsection{Community Observations, Rewards, and Learned Coordination}
\label{sec:timarl-community-learning}

Community coordination is learned from numerical aggregate observations and coupled outcomes. Typical community-scoped parts include transformer loading, remaining headroom, net community demand, photovoltaic surplus, current peak state, price multipliers, and demand-response targets. They are ordinary typed observations with explicit source, timestamp, health, and scope.

The policy receives no symbolic flexibility offer. An EV's current SoC, target SoC, departure time, charging limits, connection state, and local price or community context are sufficient for the actor to learn whether charging can be delayed or reduced. Derived features such as remaining required energy and time to departure can be included when supported by the experimental protocol. They remain observation parts and introduce no separate negotiation mechanism.

The local and community reward terms expose the trade-off to the learning algorithm. A building can reduce its own cost while contributing to a community peak, or preserve community headroom while missing an EV service target. Reporting keeps participant outcomes, community outcomes, service, constraint interventions, and distributional effects separate. A single scalar return is used for optimisation where required, with the underlying metrics still visible.

\subsection{Training Stability and Time Resolution}
\label{sec:timarl-learning-engineering}

Moving the controller to 15-minute data increased the effective learning horizon and exposed numerical failures that were less visible in the earlier hourly experiments. TI-MARL therefore treats the physical duration of a transition as part of the learner contract. When $\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}$ are declared for reference duration $\Delta t_{\mathrm{ref}}$, the factors used at runtime are

\begin{equation}
\gamma_{\Delta t}=\gamma_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\Delta t/\Delta t_{\mathrm{ref}}},
\qquad
\lambda_{\Delta t}=\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\Delta t/\Delta t_{\mathrm{ref}}}.
\label{eq:timarl-timebase}
\end{equation}

The implementation normalises critic targets, uses a Huber value loss by default, clips policy ratios and gradient norms, and records approximate KL divergence, clipping fraction, explained variance, and finite-gradient checks. Advantages can be normalised globally or per stable member identity. Entropy coefficients can differ by action-group family; a charger and a deferrable start do not need the same exploration pressure. A policy anchor can constrain movement from a selected actor and can be reset deliberately when a checkpoint is resumed.

The rollout is on-policy and retains the raw typed decision, projected decision, intervention scope, stable agent and group identities, reward decomposition, termination semantics, current and next snapshot hashes, and simulator execution feedback. Teacher actions and auxiliary-planning examples never enter that PPO rollout. This boundary matters: an update remains attributable to experience generated by the current policy even when demonstrations are used elsewhere in training.

\subsection{Reusing Prior Knowledge without Extending Runtime Authority}
\label{sec:timarl-prior-knowledge}

The work in Chapter~\ref{chap:control-benchmark} showed that a capable rule-based controller can make early training markedly less erratic. TI-MARL reuses \texttt{RBCSmart} through a typed behaviour-cloning warm start. The teacher acts on the physical simulator interface; its requested commands are decoded back into the active typed groups and stored with the exact interface snapshot on which they were valid. Pretraining updates the actor only. The critic is not trained from teacher returns, and the complete demonstration snapshots are discarded after pretraining.

Idle actions dominate many annual trajectories. A plain average cross-entropy loss can therefore learn an impressive cloning accuracy while missing the charge, discharge, V2G, or start events that make the teacher useful. The implementation supports bounded inverse-frequency weighting and a hierarchical mode mean. It can run a final unweighted calibration phase so that balancing rare actions does not permanently distort the deployed prior.

Two optional causal auxiliaries were added after the warm-start experiments exposed sparse service decisions. The EV planner labels typed states using only current service variables and declared forecasts: urgent charging, price-aware delay, authorised V2G with departure reserve, or idle. The storage planner identifies PV capture, low-price charging, high-price discharge, and idle from the same deployment-visible surface. Each auxiliary predicts both mode and fraction, trains the actor only, and keeps a bounded reservoir stratified by decision reason. Rare urgent or arbitrage examples therefore survive without contaminating the PPO rollout.

These planners are training devices, not runtime controllers. They neither issue commands nor override the analytic projector. Pure TI-MAPPO, behaviour-cloning warm start, EV auxiliary, storage auxiliary, and their combinations remain separate ablations. This separation makes the improvement over Chapter~\ref{chap:control-benchmark} testable: prior knowledge is attached to a typed and causal state, while the learned actor still owns execution.

\section{Typed Constraints, Safety, and Runtime Robustness}
\label{sec:tcg-safety}

\subsection{Typed Constraint Relations}
\label{sec:timarl-tcg}

The \gls{tcg} records the physical relations required to interpret a typed action. Its objects represent members, modules, controlled entities, action groups, resources, and constraints. A charger port carries its contribution to building import and the phases it occupies. Health and topology events can remove that contribution or change its domain.

Within a member, these relations supply the analytic projector and the trace. At community level they define what the aggregate observations, reward components, and residual violation KPIs refer to. The current set critic does not perform message passing over the TCG. This distinction prevents the data contract from being mistaken for an implemented graph-learning architecture.

For two buildings sharing one transformer, a hard constraint can be written as

\begin{equation}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}_t}
K^{\mathrm{import}}_i
\left(
\bar{a}_{i,t}
\right)
\leq
P^{\max}_{\mathrm{transformer},t}.
\label{eq:running-shared-constraint}
\end{equation}

The actors receive aggregate indications such as community loading or headroom and learn coordinated behaviour through the reward and, for TI-MAPPO, the set critic. The TCG gives the KPI and assurance layers an unambiguous definition of the resource being shared.

\subsection{Boundary of Shared-Constraint Enforcement}
\label{sec:timarl-shared-monitor}

Decentralised policies and a central training critic do not provide a hard guarantee on a constraint coupled across members. The implemented projector can guarantee only the local feasible set encoded for one member. Shared limits are represented in the simulator, exposed through authorised aggregate observations, penalised in the reward where declared, and measured from applied trajectories. A residual shared violation therefore remains a failed safety outcome, even when every local bundle was feasible.

An external minimum-correction monitor is a deployment option, not part of the evaluated v1 algorithm. Adding it would require an allocation rule for competing members and service obligations; that rule would itself affect fairness and performance. The thesis does not hide this unresolved choice behind a generic ``shield''. The final experiments report what the learned joint action achieved before any such future cross-member correction.

\subsection{Runtime Assurance and Degraded Modes}
\label{sec:timarl-runtime-assurance}

Runtime assurance handles conditions that cannot be delegated to policy quality. These include incompatible schema versions, unknown action types, compiler failure, missing safety-critical sensors, inference timeout, and actuator failure. Responses are scoped according to the dependency relations.

The compiler's concrete responses are group enablement, forced mode and fraction, dynamic bound contraction, and member isolation. A failed charger disables one group and leaves the others active. A stale SoC blocks the dependent discharge port. A failed main meter can isolate the complete member because its local limit can no longer be verified. A missing community signal retains local operation. Recovery requires the declared health and consistency conditions; one unverified sample is insufficient.

The runtime-assurance decision and the learned action remain separate records. Scientific evaluation and real-world audits can assess the policy without attributing fallback actions to it.

\section{Compatibility and Typed Explainability}
\label{sec:timarl-compatibility-explainability}

\subsection{Interface Snapshots and Admission}
\label{sec:timarl-compatibility}

Every decision is associated with an immutable interface snapshot containing active entities, observation parts, action groups, ports, validity, bounds, dependencies, health, and schema versions. The snapshot allows an experiment transition to be reconstructed even when the runtime topology subsequently changes.

Before a checkpoint is restored, its compatibility signature is checked against the current contract, schema, type registry, health rules, learning backbone, critic, actor context, and credit-assignment mode. A new instance of a known charger type can be admitted without changing network architecture. A semantically unknown type is rejected. Compiler migration requires explicit opt-in and matching contract hashes; tensor compatibility alone is insufficient.

This contract links the algorithmic method to Deployability. It prevents a model from being resumed or moved merely because tensor dimensions happen to match. The lifecycle mechanisms in Chapter~\ref{chap:lifecycle} build on the same distinction.

\subsection{Checkpoint, Export, and Actor-Only Handoff}
\label{sec:timarl-persistence}

The \texttt{ti\_marl\_checkpoint\_v1} format stores the actor, critic, optional typed-group critic, optimisers, rollout, random-number states, compiler state, behaviour-cloning state, resolved configuration, software versions, and compatibility signature. Checkpoint replacement is atomic. Restoring to a different compatible member composition is allowed because stable semantic types, rather than the active population, determine network shape.

Two exports have different purposes. The research artefact retains actor and critic state for evaluation and is marked \texttt{deployable: false}. A separate \texttt{ti\_marl\_deployment\_bundle\_v1} omits the critic and optimisation state and carries the actor, resolved typed interfaces, compiler and health rules, projector configuration, normalisation declaration, versions, and compatibility signature. This actor-only bundle defines the intended handoff to the inference lifecycle, but the current generic serving path does not yet certify it as a production TI-MARL deployment. Chapter~\ref{chap:lifecycle} preserves that boundary.

\subsection{Execution-Level Decision Traces}
\label{sec:timarl-explainability}

Typed interfaces support explanations grounded in the actual decision path. A trace can identify

\begin{itemize}
  \item the active entities, modules, and schema versions;
  \item the local and community observations available to the actor;
  \item observations that were degraded, stale, missing, or failed;
  \item the action groups and ports that existed at that step;
  \item invalid ports and the dependency or health rule that invalidated them;
  \item the port and parameter selected for each active group;
  \item any local feasibility adjustment and the active local constraint;
  \item simulator execution feedback, including requested and applied values; and
  \item the final action executed by each asset.
\end{itemize}

For example, a trace can state that \texttt{charger\_1} selected \textsc{charge-ev} while \textsc{discharge-ev} was unavailable because the bound EV SoC source was stale. It can separately record that \texttt{charger\_2} was scaled to preserve the L1 headroom and whether the simulator applied the resulting request. These statements follow compiler and constraint semantics; they are not inferred from attention weights.

Transitions are written to buffered gzip-compressed JSONL chunks. Each transition references the current and next content-addressed snapshots. Complete snapshots are deduplicated and emitted periodically or when topology and health events require them. This keeps the audit trail useful on long high-frequency runs without serialising the complete interface graph at every step.

\subsection{Formal and Operational Properties}
\label{sec:timarl-properties}

The method supports several properties whose validity can be checked independently of control performance.

\paragraph{Entity-binding integrity.}
Every observation and action instance identifies its source, owner, and target entity. The compiler rejects ambiguous or orphaned bindings.

\paragraph{Interface soundness.}
A compiled valid port has an active owner module, satisfied hard dependencies, and a recognised parameter schema. Health-dependent contraction is deterministic under a fixed snapshot.

\paragraph{Local bundle feasibility.}
The final local bundle belongs to the local feasible set when the construction layer terminates normally and the encoded constraints are correct.

\paragraph{Information-boundary preservation.}
The deployed actor consumes only local interface objects and authorised aggregate observations. Privileged critic information is excluded from the actor package.

\paragraph{Safe admission.}
Unknown or incompatible schema elements do not become executable action ports merely because a numerical embedding can be produced.

\paragraph{Trace consistency.}
The explanation record references the same interface snapshot, selected ports, constraint rules, and final action used by execution.

These properties do not prove global optimality, universal robustness, or the correctness of every physical model. They define testable invariants that separate interface and runtime correctness from empirical policy quality.

\section{Experimental Methodology and Baselines}
\label{sec:timarl-methodology}

\subsection{Comparison Design and Reference Methods}
\label{sec:timarl-comparison-design}

TI-MARL uses the information boundaries, reward lineage, scorecard, and annual datasets established in Chapters~\ref{chap:simulator} and~\ref{chap:control-benchmark}. A stable-interface comparison is required before any reconfiguration advantage is claimed. The subsequent comparisons alter one declared dimension at a time: local interface, population, health, physical pressure, or combined operation.

The executable reference set is intentionally smaller than the Chapter~\ref{chap:control-benchmark} inventory. BAU and \texttt{RBCSmart} anchor cost, service, and feasibility. TI-PPO and TI-MAPPO isolate local typed learning from centralised value estimation. The MILP oracle remains a bound on bounded scheduling windows, not a direct dynamic-interface baseline.

\subsection{Reconfiguration Scenarios, Ablations, and Metrics}
\label{sec:timarl-scenarios}

The scenarios are organised from controlled changes to integrated operation.

\paragraph{Development and stable control.}
Core-15-Stripped provides short debugging and ablation runs with a static, lightly constrained composition. Core-30-Nominal supplies the main stable-interface comparison. The same physical members and 15-minute year are retained when the experimental dimension changes.

\paragraph{Safety, health, and topology.}
Core-30-Safety increases total and per-phase pressure. Core-30-Health introduces sensor, forecast, communication, actuator, and asset evidence. Core-30-Dynamic changes members and assets, including chargers independently of their host member. Core-30-Combined overlays these dimensions and demand-response events after they have been interpreted separately.

\paragraph{Integrated confirmation.}
Premium-100-Clean retains the heterogeneous 70-residential, 20-small-service, and 10-larger-member catalogue, dynamic topology, several chargers per equipped service building, limits, services, and settlement without injected faults. Premium-100-AllIn adds coincident failure events. The AllIn run is reserved for final confirmation; it is not used to select hyperparameters.

\paragraph{Held-out semantics.}
Held-out tests use new instance counts or compositions of known types. An unknown semantic or action-group type is expected to fail admission. Calling that rejection ``zero-shot generalisation'' would weaken the claim rather than strengthen it.

Ablations alter one implemented mechanism: semantic metadata, entity binding, health closure, dynamic bounds, action-conditioned group context, analytic projection, community observations, community reward, central set critic, typed-group credit, behaviour cloning, EV planning, storage planning, deterministic decoding, or compatibility validation. The evaluation reports five evidence groups:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item control and service performance;
  \item reconfiguration and held-out-composition behaviour;
  \item Safety and Robustness, including local adjustments and assurance interventions;
  \item Scalability and runtime cost across agents, observations, groups, ports, and topology events; and
  \item Deployability and explainability, including admission, compatibility, and trace completeness.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Temporal Split, Seeds, and Selection Gates}
\label{sec:timarl-protocol}

Each annual dataset contains 35,040 action steps. Four 1,344-step development windows cover different seasons: 4032--5375, 12792--14135, 21552--22895, and 30312--31655. Four disjoint windows, 6720--8063, 15480--16823, 24240--25583, and 33000--34343, are retained for confirmation. The remaining 24,288 steps are available for training and tuning. A complete replay on the same year after training is reported as a transductive annual benchmark, not as held-out evidence.

Neural development uses seeds 123, 456, and 789. The confirmation set uses 1009, 2027, 3037, 4051, and 5059, paired with simulator seeds 2601--2604 where the scenario requires them. Checkpoint choice sees development results only. The selected policy must satisfy all of the following before cost is considered:

\begin{itemize}
  \item total applied electrical-violation energy no greater than 0.5~kWh;
  \item at least 99\% of feasible EV departures meeting minimum acceptable service;
  \item no decrease in feasible EV target-tolerance rate or deferrable service relative to the paired reference;
  \item no more than 5\% deterioration in daily peak ratio or ramping ratio;
  \item no more than two percentage points loss in solar self-consumption.
\end{itemize}

Among policies that pass, promotion requires at least 0.1\% cost improvement. Daily peak, ramping, and solar self-consumption resolve ties in that order. The final scorecard also reports all-time peak, load factor, battery throughput, V2G export, community import/export/net exchange, members that improve, training time, and deterministic inference latency. Failing a hard gate cannot be offset by a lower scalar cost.

\section{Discussion, Generality, and Contribution to the Research Questions}
\label{sec:timarl-discussion}

TI-MARL extends the methods of \cref{chap:control-benchmark} by changing the object over which the controller operates. The actor no longer assumes one fixed list of assets and action coordinates; it acts over an entity-bound interface compiled from the modules, sessions, health evidence, and bounds that exist now. TI-MAPPO's critic no longer requires a permanent concatenated population; it evaluates the active member set. The same semantics support local constraints, checkpoint compatibility, admission, and decision traces.

RQ1 is addressed by stable member, charger, EV, and session identities; variable observation and action-group sets; known-type admission; topology-aware transitions; and a shared actor that preserves local information boundaries. The member can gain or lose a charger without inventing a new output coordinate, while the policy remains responsible for the active local bundle.

RQ2 is addressed conditionally. Shared type heads and set encoders keep the parameter count independent of the number of known instances. Packed evaluation and cached structural preparation reduce repeated work. The central critic accepts a variable population. None of these makes computation constant: tokens, groups, compiler work, trace volume, and sample requirements still grow and are measured explicitly.

RQ3 is addressed by causal health derivation, dependency closure, forced typed modes, dynamic bounds, EV and deferrable service floors, total and per-phase projection, and requested--projected--applied evidence. The guarantee is local and conditional on the encoded physical model. Shared community safety remains an empirical outcome because v1 has no cross-member hard corrector.

RQ4 receives the algorithm-side contract: resolved interfaces, content-addressed snapshots, compatibility hashes, topology-aware checkpoints, actor-only handoff, and execution traces. These records make governance possible; they do not implement promotion, distributed rollout, monitoring, or rollback, which are developed in \cref{chap:lifecycle}.

Typed explainability is a cross-cutting outcome. Mode identities, entity bindings, health rules, bounds, local adjustments, and simulator execution feedback are part of the executable contract. They can be reported directly, without translating anonymous output indices after the decision. This does not explain the internal causal reasoning of the network; it provides a faithful account of what the policy could observe and invoke, what the compiler excluded, and how the final command was obtained.

The formal model can represent modular robots, industrial systems, edge services, and other CPSs with changing capabilities and local/shared resources. The evidence in this thesis remains energy-specific. Generality applies to the abstraction and implementation pattern. Performance in another domain requires new experiments, constraints, and safety evidence.

The principal limitations are concrete. Known semantic and action-group families remain necessary. Mean-pooled community context cannot represent every feeder or phase topology. Aggregate signals can be delayed or insufficient. The projector is only as correct as its electrical and service contract, and repeated intervention can hide weak policy learning unless raw actions are reported. Auxiliary planners encode useful causal heuristics but can bias the policy towards their coverage. The current actor-only handoff is not yet a certified production serving path. Most importantly, final control and generalisation claims still depend on the multi-seed confirmation campaign.

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:timarl-summary}

TI-MARL treats the current observation--action interface of each agent as a versioned, entity-bound runtime object. The Typed Interface Compiler derives observation parts, dynamic action-group instances, parameterised ports, health-dependent validity, bounds, dependencies, and local constraint relations from the active modules and entities. A local policy encodes the variable interface into one shared local representation and jointly decodes an action bundle across the controllable groups that currently exist. New instances reuse the same type-conditioned decoders instead of requiring permanent asset-specific heads.

The method is multi-agent through the joint physical transition, coupled local and community outcomes, shared resources, dynamic population, and TI-MAPPO's central set critic. Execution remains decentralised: each actor receives its local interface and authorised aggregate community numbers. The analytic projector applies validity, service, and local electrical constraints. Health events change the interface at the appropriate scope, from one port to the complete member.

This structure links the controller progression to the four requirements of the thesis. Dynamic heterogeneity becomes an interface and transition problem; scalability is tied to parameter sharing and measured variable-cardinality cost; safety and robustness acquire explicit health and feasibility semantics; deployability begins with compatibility, persistence, and trace contracts. The following chapter moves from that algorithm-side boundary to the lifecycle required to reproduce, validate, package, deploy, monitor, update, and recover policies and policy sets.

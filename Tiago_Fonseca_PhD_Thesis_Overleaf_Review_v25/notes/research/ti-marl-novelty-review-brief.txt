PROPOSED CONCEPT

The proposed method concerns multi-agent reinforcement learning for
heterogeneous and dynamically reconfigurable cyber-physical systems.

At each decision step, every active agent exposes a runtime Agent Schema
that may contain:

- agent identity and role;
- installed modules and capabilities;
- sensors and observation sources;
- actuators and callable action capabilities;
- local physical and operational constraints;
- status and health information;
- timing, communication, and computational properties;
- dependencies between observations, action capabilities, constraints,
  and validity conditions.

The Agent Schema generates a variable set of typed observation parts and
typed action ports.

A typed observation part may include:

- semantic type;
- value or structured value;
- unit and shape;
- scope: self, local, community, or global;
- source and provenance;
- timestamp and age;
- quality, confidence, or uncertainty;
- validity and missingness state;
- dependencies and relationships with other observations or actions.

A typed action port may include:

- semantic name and action family;
- discrete type or mode;
- parameter schema;
- continuous or discrete parameter domains;
- units and physical bounds;
- dynamic bounds;
- validity predicate;
- required observations or capabilities;
- resource-consumption or coupling metadata;
- safety class and fallback behaviour;
- expected physical effect or effect model.

The policy does not emit through one fixed universal action vector.
Instead, it:

1. encodes the current Agent Schema;
2. encodes the current typed observation parts;
3. encodes the currently available typed action ports;
4. produces a shared latent representation;
5. scores or selects one or more current action ports;
6. generates port-specific parameters;
7. produces a local action proposal.

The method must also support multiple agents whose individually valid
actions are jointly incompatible. For example, two EV chargers may each
propose 0.6 charging power while the shared site limit is 1.0.

A joint coordination and safety layer therefore receives:

- local action proposals;
- local policy scores, Q-values, priorities, or utilities;
- participant objectives;
- community objectives;
- local constraints;
- shared physical and resource constraints;
- fairness and service requirements.

It returns jointly feasible and coordinated executed actions. The
mechanism should do more than clipping: it should decide how scarce
capacity is allocated based on urgency, expected utility, fairness,
community objectives, and physical limits.

The method should support:

- heterogeneous agents and asset types;
- agents joining and leaving;
- modules or capabilities appearing and disappearing;
- observation and action interfaces changing at runtime;
- missing, stale, uncertain, or failed sensors;
- graceful degradation and fail-safe operation;
- multi-dimensional scalability;
- explicit decision traces and interface-level explainability;
- deployment through versioned and machine-readable interface contracts.

REVIEW OBJECTIVES

Determine whether prior work already provides any of the following:

A. A formal Markov-game or related model in which each active agent has
   a runtime schema that induces both observations and actions.

B. A policy that operates directly over variable sets of semantically
   typed action candidates rather than over a fixed universal head.

C. Dynamic parameterised action ports with individual parameter schemas,
   bounds, validity predicates, and dependencies on observation sources.

D. Explicit relations between sensor health and action-port validity or
   parameter bounds.

E. Runtime interface contraction or expansion when capabilities,
   observations, or modules change.

F. A multi-agent architecture combining dynamic typed interfaces with
   centralised training and decentralised execution.

G. Joint correction or optimisation of locally proposed actions under
   coupled physical constraints and community objectives.

H. A mechanism that simultaneously provides feasibility, intelligent
   allocation, fairness, and minimum deviation from the local policies.

I. Policy compatibility and deployment semantics derived from machine-
   readable observation and action schemas.

J. Interface-level explainability, including selected action port,
   rejected or invalid ports, validity reasons, supporting observations,
   parameter bounds, safety corrections, active global constraints, and
   coordination adjustments.

Test these candidate claims independently:

N1. Runtime agent schemas induce both typed observation parts and typed
    parameterised action ports.

N2. The policy selects and parameterises action ports from the current
    runtime interface, rather than emitting through a fixed universal
    output head.

N3. Observation health and semantic dependencies modify action-port
    validity and bounds at runtime.

    population changes without rebuilding the global policy layout.

    feasible and utility-aware actions under shared physical constraints.
